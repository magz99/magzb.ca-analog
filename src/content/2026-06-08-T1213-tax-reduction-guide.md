# T1213: How to apply for the income tax reduction at source

I first heard about the T1213 form from a Youtube video or Instagram post. It mentioned that you could fill out this form to reduce the taxes that come out of your paycheque so that you could then deposit that amount into a savings account or investment account. That amount, which you accumulate over the year, could gain you a bit of interest. This is money that the government would have to return back to you after you file your taxes, so why let the government hold onto your money interest-free?

Okay, so that seemed like a compelling argument: you are letting someone hold onto a sum of money (potentially thousands) for you interest free, for almost 16 months*. For example, on $5,000 sitting in a HISA at 3.5%, that's roughly $175 you're leaving on the table. That’s a win right?

But what do I need to do to get that money? In comes the form T1213, also known as “Request to Reduce Tax Deductions at Source”, which you can search for on the [Canada.ca](http://Canada.ca) website. Download and go through the form first, to see if it applies to you.

*16 months is assuming from the start of the calendar year until the following year, when you file your taxes and get your return in April.

## Do I even qualify?

I’ll be speaking anecdotally about this, but in general, if you get a tax refund every year, there's a good chance you qualify. In my scenario, I focus on RRSP contributions, because they lower your taxable income, which means you may get a tax refund come tax time. It’s also one of the applicable deductions in the form, but note that it must be contributions that you made *in addition* to the contributions that come out of your paycheque (if you have employer RRSP matching for example). Other applicable deductions may include: employment expenses, child care expenses, support payments, medical expenses, donations, etc. 

In my case, I knew that RRSP contributions, employment expenses, and donations would help me qualify.  Note that each line in the form does ask you for supporting documentation, which I will discuss in the next section.

## Quick Reference Timeline

- **After tax season (April–May):** gather supporting documents
- **By September 1st:** submit T1213 via My CRA
- **6 weeks later:** receive letter of authority
- **Before January:** hand letter to payroll
- **New year:** reduced withholding begins

## How do I fill out this form?

The form has 4 parts: personal info, payment type, your deductions, and a certification signature. Once you’ve got an idea of which sections you may qualify for, it’s time to get your supporting documentation. Note that you are filling out the form for the NEXT calendar year, and your supporting documents will be for the CURRENT calendar year (think of it as: use last year's proof to justify next year's reduction). So for this article I am writing in 2026, I am filling out the form for 2027, and will provide supporting documents from the April 2026 tax season (which is technically the 2025 tax year). The supporting documents will help the CRA determine the final amount you’ll be approved for.

**RRSP**

The form mentions “details or a copy of the payment arrangement contract”

I asked the investment company that I make contributions to to send me a letter that includes instruction details for that RRSP account. In this case it was a Portfolio report which target my RRSP, it included the amount, frequency and instruction type (PAC - Pre-authorized contribution).

**Employment Expenses**

This section mentions two forms: T2200 and T777. Form T2200 I have been filling out since COVID due to working remotely. This is a form you can generally submit at tax time, as it indicates that you are working from home.  Your employer does need to sign this form before you submit it at tax time. So you may already have it handy from last year’s tax submission. The other form is Form T777 which is a statement of your employment expenses. If you work from home, then you can include expenses such as internet, heating, electricity, but relative to the size of your dedicated office-space within your home (not whole-house expenses 😁). 

Just a note that since 2023, the CRA has tightened the work-from-home expense rules significantly: the flat-rate method was eliminated and you now need the T2200 + T777 forms regardless.

**Donations**

I donate to some animal charities throughout the year, some are pre-authorized monthly contributions, others are one-off donations. Make sure you keep those receipts handy. If you submitted your donation receipts during tax season, then those same receipts can be used as supporting documentation for this section of the form.

**Last Section (and potential gotcha)**

The last section of the form mentions “Other income on which payers will make no tax deductions at source”. In my case, I did have some additional income, let’s say $20000 that I wasn’t taxed on (which I did declare during tax season as well). So it’s better that you declare it here so it matches. This will definitely affect the final amount you are approved for, if you are even approved at all. The CRA essentially wouldn’t want to approve a $10,000 reduction if you also have $8,000 in income they haven't touched yet. 

If you had a supporting tax form you used during tax season, that would be the form you attach as part of your supporting documents.

## Filled Out T1213 Form (Example)

This is part of the form that I filled out in **2025**, using documents from the **2025** tax season. The approved amount will apply towards my **2026** paycheques.

### Part 1

Straightforward: you fill out your personal information, employer’s name and contact information.

### Part 2

In my case, I selected Salary since I am expecting this deduction to be applied throughout my pay periods, not a lump sum.

## Submitting Your Documents

Now that you’ve filled out and signed the T1213 form and gathered your supporting documents, you are ready to submit them through the My CRA portal.

Once you’ve logged into My CRA portal, on the left menu there will be a link called “Submit Documents”.  There will be a dropdown to select the topic, select the topic "Contact Centre and International Correspondence”. Yes it does feel like the wrong category, but it’s the correct one! You’ll upload your form and the supporting documents. You also have the option to mail a physical copy to the CRA, see the bottom of the T1213 for more details.

Once you’ve submitted, you’ll receive an email from the CRA with a confirmation number and a case reference number. 

Remember to submit everything by September 1st to give the CRA enough time to assess your situation and send you the tax deduction letter a few weeks later. For me, I believe it took about 6 weeks to get the letter. You want to ideally provide this letter to your employer a few weeks before the start of the next calendar year.

## Letter of Authority

After a few weeks you should receive a letter in the mail which will inform you if you were approved for the tax deduction, and what amount was approved. 

In my case, they did not approve the sum of all the sections I filled out, again because I had some additional income that was not yet taxed, so that counted against the final amount. 

The final portion of the letter should be scanned and given to your employer. The page will mention “Note to the employer or payer:” and there will be instructions for Payroll software or if the employer calculates manually.

- **F1 value** → for payroll software (the approved amount gets built into the formula)
- **Manual calculation** → divide by pay periods, subtract from gross each cycle

## Next Steps

Once you get the letter, scan it, and send it to your company’s payroll department. It may take them a few weeks to get it in their system (especially if they don’t get this request very often).

You may get some pushback from your employer, especially if they haven’t done this before. It’s important that you advocate for yourself and let them know that they are obligated to do so once they’ve receive the letter of authority from you. Check out this section in the [canada.ca](http://canada.ca) website for more information on the Employer’s steps: [https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/set-up-new-employee/increase-reduce-income-tax-deducted-source.html#h-2](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/set-up-new-employee/increase-reduce-income-tax-deducted-source.html#h-2). Try to keep your correspondence in writing (email, paper trail) and feel free to send them the link above as well.

So in my case, my employer's payroll department took a little while to get it right. They initially entered the approved amount, which I could see in my paystub, but my net pay was still the same as before. After some back and forth, they eventually got it sorted out with the third-party payroll system they were using. That's not uncommon, and it just goes to show how rarely people apply for the tax reduction! So when you get your paystub, check that your taxable earnings line has decreased, not just that a new deduction line appeared.

One important point I want to mention about the amount you are approved for. *This is the amount that gets deducted from your gross pay*. So it’s not something that gets added on to your net pay, which is what I initially thought. So the $5700, divided by 26 pay periods (if you’re bi-weekly) comes out to roughly $219. So if your *gross* pay is 3000, it’ll now be $2781 that you are taxed on. Because that $219 is taxed at your marginal rate before it reaches your pocket, the actual net increase is smaller: in this example it’s roughly $120 more per paycheque rather than the full $219.

Lastly, this “extra” money that you now receive each paycheque should ideally not be spent. *Remember, the goal was never to get more money, it was to stop lending yours to the government for free*. Technically, if you are still making the similar RRSP or other contributions that initially got you approved, then sure. As long as it’s earning you even 1%, that’s still better than letting it sit interest-free in the CRA’s hands until tax season. *The T1213 accelerates your refund into your regular paycheques. It doesn't change the total amount you save, just the timing.* If you're not sure where to put that extra amount, a TFSA is a good place to start.
